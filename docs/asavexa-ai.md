# ASAVEXA AI

Four modes, all read-only, all grounded in the organisation's own records. Page: `#/ai` ("ASAVEXA AI"). Needs the LEDGER_READ permission.

| Mode | Question | Endpoint |
|---|---|---|
| AI Explain | Why was this transaction classified this way? | `POST /ai/explain` |
| AI Detect | Find unusual transactions. | `POST /ai/detect` |
| AI Recommend | Suggest a reconciliation or adjustment. | `POST /ai/recommend` |
| AI Prove | Show the evidence supporting your answer. | `POST /ai/prove` |
| Ask (free text, routed to a mode or refused) | any | `POST /ai/ask` |

## Every answer item is the same grounded chain
AI conclusion -> Source records -> Evidence -> Journal -> Accounting treatment -> Reporting framework -> Confidence -> Human review requirement.
A stage that does not apply says why (`available: false`); missing evidence is shown as MISSING EVIDENCE. The server validates the chain before answering; if it cannot ground an answer it refuses (`grounded: false`) instead of guessing.

## Confidence and human review
Starts at 100 and loses points for each weakness (unverified evidence, no evidence, odd debit/credit direction, wording hint mismatch ...). HIGH >= 80, MEDIUM 50-79, LOW < 50. Anything below HIGH always requires human review.

## What it is, honestly
- Deterministic rules over your records. No outside AI model. Same records -> same answer and fingerprint.
- Detect: published rules (shown on the page); outliers use median/MAD.
- Recommend: proposals only (`applied: false`); a person drafts, evidences and approves any entry.
- Prove: PROVEN means the records support the claim, not that documents are authentic.
- Wording checks are keyword hints. It explains the account choice that was recorded; it does not choose accounts.
- Suggested standards are defaults, not legal advice.
- Each query is audited as `AI_<MODE>` and kept out of the Passport audit trail.

## ASAVEXA AI on every page (the "Proof Lens")

A gold-and-navy **Seal** sits at the bottom-right of every signed-in page. Opening it shows a ruled-paper side panel (a bottom sheet on a phone) for the workspace the person is in.

- **Brief tab.** A short list of what is waiting in that workspace (for example "1 vulnerability is past its fix deadline"), numbered like ledger folios, urgent first. It is built only from that workspace's own overview, fetched with the person's own login, so the server's permission checks apply. A gold-red drop on the Seal shows how many items need attention. Each line links to the records. The brief shows its source and the time it was read, and has a Refresh button.
- **Ask tab.** Free-text questions go to the same grounded engine as the full page (`POST /ai/ask`). Each answer card shows a confidence stamp and a **proof thread**: source records, evidence, journal, accounting treatment, reporting framework. A solid bead is a link that exists; a hollow bead says the link is missing; half-filled means the evidence is only uploaded, not verified. Anything below HIGH confidence says a person must review it. A question that cannot be grounded is refused, not guessed.
- **Workspaces covered:** Financial Analysis, Tax Compliance, Forensic Investigation, GRC, Public Finance, Business Development, Cybersecurity, Health Centre, and the shared finance pages (evidence brief).
- **Who sees it:** anyone who can ask (`ledger:read`) or open at least one workspace. Donors, regulators and investor reviewers do not get the Seal. A role without `ledger:read` gets the brief but a locked Ask tab. The panel never shows another workspace's or organisation's brief, even while loading.
- **Keyboard and screen readers:** the Seal is a button with a name and `aria-expanded`; opening moves focus into the panel; Escape closes it and returns focus to the Seal; dark mode and reduced motion are respected.

### What it is not
- Still rule-based. No outside AI model; the brief counts items and does not judge whether they are correct.
- It adds nothing to the backend: no new endpoints and no stored data. Asking a question is audited as before (`AI_*`); opening a brief is an ordinary read of the workspace overview.
- The health brief covers the facility you are on (or the first you can see); claims figures stay hidden from roles without claims access.
