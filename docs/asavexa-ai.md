# ASAVEXA AI

Four modes, all read-only, all grounded in the organisation's own records. Page: `#/ai` ("ASAVEXA AI"). Needs the LEDGER_READ permission.

| Mode | Question | Endpoint |
|---|---|---|
| AI Explain | Why was this transaction classified this way? | `POST /ai/explain` |
| AI Detect | Find unusual transactions. | `POST /ai/detect` |
| AI Recommend | Suggest a reconciliation or adjustment. | `POST /ai/recommend` |
| AI Prove | Show the evidence supporting your answer. | `POST /ai/prove` |
| Ask (free text, routed to a mode or refused) | any | `POST /ai/ask` |

## Every answer item is the same grounded chain
AI conclusion -> Source records -> Evidence -> Journal -> Accounting treatment -> Reporting framework -> Confidence -> Human review requirement.
A stage that does not apply says why (`available: false`); missing evidence is shown as MISSING EVIDENCE. The server validates the chain before answering; if it cannot ground an answer it refuses (`grounded: false`) instead of guessing.

## Confidence and human review
Starts at 100 and loses points for each weakness (unverified evidence, no evidence, odd debit/credit direction, wording hint mismatch ...). HIGH >= 80, MEDIUM 50-79, LOW < 50. Anything below HIGH always requires human review.

## What it is, honestly
- Deterministic rules over your records. No outside AI model. Same records -> same answer and fingerprint.
- Detect: published rules (shown on the page); outliers use median/MAD.
- Recommend: proposals only (`applied: false`); a person drafts, evidences and approves any entry.
- Prove: PROVEN means the records support the claim, not that documents are authentic.
- Wording checks are keyword hints. It explains the account choice that was recorded; it does not choose accounts.
- Suggested standards are defaults, not legal advice.
- Each query is audited as `AI_<MODE>` and kept out of the Passport audit trail.
